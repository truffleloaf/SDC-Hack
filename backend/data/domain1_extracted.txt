AI is changing which jobs exist faster than training
programmes and skills frameworks can be updated. In
Malaysia, 56% of professionals already use AI tools at work,
yet only 26% have received formal training (Hays Asia Salary
Guide 2025). The Ministry of Science, Technology and
Innovation has said that more than 600,000 workers will
need reskilling within three to five years.
Aiversecity believes AI should be a partner to people, not a
replacement for them. Today, workers do not know which AI
skills to learn, employers cannot tell which roles to train
their people for, and learning pathways are usually static
lists that are out of date by the time they are published.
Because AI progress is unpredictable, a one-time skills
assessment is not enough: we need a system that keeps
learning as the technology moves.
Future-ready skills are also sustainability skills. Many of
tomorrow’s jobs, in clean energy, health, smart agriculture
and responsible AI, are the ones that will help deliver the 17
UN Sustainable Development Goals.

3.
Build a working web app or dashboard (a proof of concept is
acceptable) that takes a user from “who I am today” to “where
I can be in the AI era”. A typical journey looks like this:
What to Build
1.Tell us about yourself. The user enters their skills,
experience and interests through a CV upload, form or short
questionnaire.
2.See the Future Skills Radar. The system shows which skills
and roles are rising or declining, based on identifiable AI-
trend and job-market signals.
3.Get matched. The system recommends best-fit future roles,
each with a match score and a skills-gap breakdown.
4.Follow a pathway. The user receives a step-by-step
reskilling plan, with every recommended role and skill
linked to one or more of the 17 SDGs.

6. Rules and Boundaries
Data: use public, open or synthetic data only. Do not use real
personal data or real CVs; create sample profiles for your demo.
AI disclosure and guardrails: if you use a language model, say
which one. Every recommendation must cite a source, and the
system must not invent jobs, courses or statistics.
Human-centred: position your solution as AI that supports
human career decisions, not one that replaces human
judgement or makes final hiring decisions.
Technology: free choice of stack.
Out of scope: creating full course content, payment systems
and real recruitment integrations.

8. Marking Rubric – Preliminary Round
The preliminary round judges your proposed idea and a first proof that
you can build it. You do not need a finished product, but you do need a
rough, working version of your single most important feature, shown
live and backed by your GitHub repository.
